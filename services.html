A starting point that fits the work.

These are ways to structure an engagement, not fixed packages. Deliverables, exclusions, fees, and milestones are agreed for your project before work begins.

01

Product discovery

For an idea or operating problem that needs a clearer brief before development.

Possible outputs
A requirements brief, workflow map, technical approach, and a proposed build scope. A prototype can be included where it helps resolve a specific question.
A clearer decision about what to build next.
02

Focused build

For a defined website, feature, prototype, or integration with a contained objective.

Possible outputs
The agreed product surface or feature, relevant integration work, and testing against its acceptance criteria. Existing-system access and dependencies are scoped first.
A bounded piece of work with a clear finish.
03

End-to-end delivery

For a new platform or internal tool that needs definition, interface work, and engineering together.

Possible outputs
Agreed user journeys, application interfaces, back-end services where needed, testing, and a deployment and handover plan.
One connected scope from brief to release.
04

Product continuation

For an existing product with a new feature backlog or maintenance needs.

Possible outputs
An agreed improvement backlog, scoped fixes or features, and release notes. Codebase review, availability, and ongoing support terms are discussed before committing.
A next phase—not an automatic support subscription.

How delivery moves.

Every scope is different. The working rhythm stays visible and direct.

  1. 01

    Frame

    Share the problem, who uses the product, your current workflow, and any deadline or budget constraints. An existing system or rough brief is enough to start the discussion.

    Checkpoint A shared brief and the questions that still need answering.

  2. 02

    Shape

    Define the proposed solution, deliverables, exclusions, dependencies, and review milestones. Agree the commercial terms and what completion means before the build starts.

    Checkpoint An agreed scope and acceptance criteria.

  3. 03

    Build

    Review working increments at the agreed milestones. Feedback is checked against the scope; new requirements are discussed for their effect on cost and timing before being added.

    Checkpoint Reviewed functionality and a record of open issues.

  4. 04

    Run

    Review the completed scope, resolve agreed release issues, and carry out the planned deployment and handover. Any maintenance or next phase is agreed separately.

    Checkpoint A release and handover reviewed against the agreed scope.

Plan the handover, not just the launch.

The handover checklist is defined with the project. Depending on scope, it can cover:

  • Repository and deployment access, with ownership and licences clarified.
  • Setup notes, configuration requirements, and essential operating guidance.
  • A walkthrough, known limitations, and any separately agreed follow-up work.

Before we begin.

A few things worth making explicit. Project-specific terms belong in the agreed scope, not in assumptions.

What should I send for the first conversation?

Describe the problem, the people affected, and what a useful result would look like. Add your existing website or system, examples, and any budget range or target date you already have. You do not need a polished specification.

Please keep passwords, API keys, and private customer records out of the enquiry form.

How are pricing and timelines decided?

They depend on the agreed scope, integrations, available content, review needs, and technical dependencies. Fees, payment milestones, and the proposed schedule are discussed before work starts; there is no universal price or turnaround listed here.

How are feedback and revisions handled?

Review checkpoints and revision allowances are agreed for the project. A correction against the accepted scope is distinguished from a new feature or change of direction. Changes are discussed for their cost and schedule impact before proceeding.

Who owns the code, accounts, and final assets?

Ownership, source-code access, account responsibilities, and handover requirements need to be confirmed in the project agreement. Third-party software and assets may have their own licence terms; custom work and external dependencies should be identified separately.

Are hosting and third-party services included?

Hosting, domains, email delivery, app-store accounts, and paid APIs are identified during scoping where relevant. Which costs are included, who holds each account, and who pays recurring charges are agreed before those services are set up.

What happens after launch?

Any post-launch fixes, maintenance, monitoring, response expectations, and future development are defined in the agreed support scope. Ongoing support is not automatically included in every build; it can be discussed as a separate continuation engagement.
